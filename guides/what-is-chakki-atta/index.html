What is chakki atta?

Atta is the whole wheat flour Indian kitchens use for rotis. “Chakki” tells you how it was milled. Here is what that means in your kitchen.

By the Samriddhi Bhog team · 3 October 2026

Atta, wheat flour and whole wheat flour

In India, atta means flour milled from the whole wheat grain — the bran, the germ and the endosperm, all ground together. It is the flour behind everyday rotis, phulkas, parathas and puris.

“Wheat flour” on its own is a broader label. It can mean atta, but refined flour (maida) is also made from wheat. The ingredients list settles it: a pack of whole wheat atta should say whole wheat.

What makes atta “chakki” atta

A chakki is a mill that grinds grain between two stones. The whole wheat kernel goes in and comes out as flour, with the bran and germ ground in along with the rest of the grain.

Large roller mills work differently: steel rollers break the grain in stages and can separate the bran, germ and endosperm. Whole wheat flour from a roller mill is made by putting those parts back together.

How chakki atta behaves in the kitchen

  • It feels slightly granular between your fingers rather than powder-smooth.
  • It takes up more water when you knead, so add water a little at a time.
  • The colour is cream to light brown, with fine specks of bran.
  • Resting the dough for 15–20 minutes makes it easier to roll and gives softer rotis.

Frequently asked questions

Is chakki atta the same as whole wheat flour?

+

Yes. Both are flour from the whole wheat grain. “Chakki” describes the milling method — grinding between stones.

Why does chakki atta need more water?

+

Because it is slightly coarser and contains the bran, it absorbs more water while kneading. Add water gradually until the dough is soft.

Can I use chakki atta for parathas and puris?

+

Yes. Chakki atta is the standard flour for rotis, parathas and puris in Indian homes.
